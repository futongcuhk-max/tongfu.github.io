01 — About

Who I Am &
What I Study

I am a Ph.D. researcher in the Photonics Group at the Department of Physics, City University of Hong Kong, supervised by Prof. Shubo Wang. My research is focused on the topological and geometric properties of optical near fields.

My current work centers on near-field topological photonics — extending concepts like the Chern number and Euler characteristic from momentum-space band theory into the real-space electromagnetic fields surrounding finite nanostructures. A key result is that the real-space spin Chern number of optical near fields is quantized and equals the structure's Euler characteristic, independent of material or excitation.

Beyond topology, I study polarization singularities and optical spin-orbit interactions, including C-lines, dark spots, transverse spin, and spin-momentum locking in near-field environments. I am interested in how these phenomena can be harnessed for chiral light-matter interaction, optical manipulation, and topological sensing.

02 — Research

Areas of
Investigation

01

Topological Photonics

Extending topological invariants — spin Chern numbers, Euler characteristics, polarization singularities — from momentum-space band theory into the real-space near fields of finite nanostructures. Our framework reveals how the shape of a structure topologically constrains its electromagnetic near field.

Chern NumberNear-Field Topology C-LinesEuler Characteristic
02

Spin Photonics

Investigating spin-orbit interactions of light in nanoscale environments: transverse spin, spin-momentum locking, and the photonic spin Hall effect. We study how photon spin couples to orbital angular momentum in evanescent fields and near resonant structures, enabling directional emission and chiral near fields.

Transverse SpinSpin-Orbit Spin-Momentum LockingEvanescent Fields
03

Nanophotonics

Light-matter interaction at the nanoscale: topological dark spots, C-lines and polarization singularities in near fields of metallic nanostructures, optical forces on chiral and spinning particles, and the design of nanostructures with tailored topological near-field properties.
